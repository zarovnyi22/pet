# Milk 2.5% Fat

**Supplier grade:** pasteurised cow's milk, standardised to 2.5% fat, 2.9% protein. Stored at 2–6 °C, used within 72 h of opening. Main base for set and stirred yogurts, kefir and fermented desserts.

## Function in fermented dairy

- **Fermentation substrate:** lactose (4.7%) is the sugar the yogurt cultures convert to lactic acid; about 20–30% of it is fermented, so finished yogurt has roughly 3.3–3.8 g lactose per 100 g of milk used.
- **Gel structure:** casein (about 80% of the protein) coagulates at pH 4.6 and forms the yogurt gel. Protein below 2.8% gives a weak set and whey separation.
- **Mouthfeel:** 2.5% fat gives a medium-creamy texture; below 1.5% fat add 1–2% skimmed milk powder to keep viscosity.

## Typical dosage

| Product | Dosage |
|---|---|
| Fruit yogurt (base) | 75–85% of formula |
| Plain set yogurt | 95–98% of formula |
| Kefir, fermented drinks | 90–97% of formula |

## Substitutes in fermented products

| Substitute | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Soy drink, unsweetened (spec-soy-drink) | 1:1 by weight | closest protein (3.0 g), gels well with plant cultures; introduces **soy** allergen |
| Oat drink (spec-oat-drink) | 1:1 by weight | only 1.0 g protein: weak gel, add 1–2% starch or pectin; oats = gluten unless certified GF |
| Coconut drink | 1:1 by weight | very low protein (0.2–0.5 g), needs thickener; creamy flavour |

## Cautions

- Plant drinks contain no lactose. Dairy cultures cannot acidify them without an added sugar (1–2% sucrose or glucose) — see spec-yogurt-starter.
- Replacing milk does not make the product milk-free if the starter was grown on dairy media (spec-yogurt-starter).
- A milk-to-soy or milk-to-oat swap drops protein per 100 g of yogurt; check "source of protein" claims.
