# Steviol Glycosides, Rebaudioside A 97% (E960a)

**Supplier grade:** white powder, rebaudioside A ≥ 97%, extracted from *Stevia rebaudiana* leaves.

## Function

- **Sweetness only:** 200–300 times sweeter than sucrose. It adds **no bulk, no browning and no tenderness**, so it must always be combined with a bulking agent (spec-erythritol, spec-polydextrose).
- **Heat stability:** stable up to 200 °C and between pH 3 and 9; suitable for baking and pasteurised drinks.
- **Taste profile:** sweetness is slower to start and lingers. Above about 0.05% of product weight a bitter, liquorice aftertaste appears.

## Typical dosage

| Use | Dosage (% of product weight) |
|---|---|
| Cookies, cakes (with bulking agent) | 0.01–0.03% |
| Muffins | 0.01–0.02% |
| Beverages | 0.02–0.05% |
| Upper sensory limit | 0.05% |

Rule of thumb: **0.01% Reb A replaces the sweetness of about 2.5–3% sucrose.** For example, removing 48 g sugar from a 700 g cookie dough (6.9%) and adding 27 g erythritol (sweetness ≈ 17.5 g sugar) leaves a gap of ≈ 30 g sugar-equivalent; full compensation would need about 0.1 g Reb A. In trial-cookies-sugar-30 only 0.06 g was used per 700 g dough (partial compensation, ≈ 18 g sugar-equivalent), and 68% of panelists still rated sweetness "just right".

Weigh stevia as a 1:100 pre-blend with erythritol or polydextrose; weighing 0.06 g directly on a production scale is not accurate enough.

## Regulatory

- Acceptable daily intake: 4 mg/kg body weight per day, expressed as steviol equivalents (EFSA). Rebaudioside A is about 33% steviol.
- Declared as "sweetener (steviol glycosides)" or E960a.

## Substitutes

- Sucralose (E955, 600×): cleaner taste, but customers see it as "artificial"; not used in our clean-label range.
- Monk fruit extract: authorised in the EU only as a flavouring preparation, not as a sweetener.

## Cautions

- Never use stevia alone as a sugar replacement in bakery; volume and texture collapse because the sugar mass is missing.
- Masking: 0.1–0.2% vanilla or 0.05% salt reduces bitterness.
