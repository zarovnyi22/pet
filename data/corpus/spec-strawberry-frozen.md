# Strawberries, Frozen (IQF), Unsweetened

**Supplier grade:** whole or diced strawberries, individually quick frozen, no added sugar. Stored at −18 °C or below; thawed product is used within 24 h at 2–6 °C.

## Function

- **Fruit preparation:** main fruit in strawberry yogurts, fillings and smoothies; usually cooked into a fruit preparation (fruit + sugar + pectin) before being added to the yogurt base.
- **Flavour and colour:** anthocyanins give the red colour; they fade above pH 4.5 and on long heating. Keep cooking to 85–90 °C for 2–5 minutes.
- **Sweetness:** natural sugars (4.6 g per 100 g) are about half glucose/fructose, half sucrose; they count toward total sugars on the label.

## Typical dosage

| Product | Dosage |
|---|---|
| Fruit yogurt | 8–15% of formula |
| Fruit preparation | 40–60% of preparation |
| Smoothies | 20–40% |

## Allergens

No EU Annex II allergens. Suitable for vegan products. Check supplier declarations for cross-contact on lines shared with nuts.

## Nutrients per 100 g

| Nutrient | Value |
|---|---|
| Energy | 35 kcal |
| Protein | 0.4 g |
| Fat | 0.1 g |
| Carbohydrates | 9.1 g |
| of which sugars | 4.6 g |
| Fibre | 2.1 g |

## Sugar reduction notes

- Strawberries themselves contribute little sugar: 100 g in 1 kg of yogurt adds only 0.46 g sugars per 100 g of product. In a sugar-reduction project, the added sucrose is the main lever, not the fruit (guideline-sugar-reduction).
- Increasing fruit from 10% to 15% raises perceived sweetness and fruitiness and allows 5–10% less added sugar at equal liking.
- Sweetened frozen strawberries (typically 1 part sugar to 4–5 parts fruit) contain about 20 g sugars per 100 g — do not confuse the two grades in calculations.

## Substitutes

| Substitute | Notes |
|---|---|
| Strawberry puree, unsweetened | same nutrients, smoother texture, no visible fruit pieces |
| Freeze-dried strawberry powder | about 10× concentrated: 1 g powder ≈ 10 g fruit; sugars about 45 g per 100 g powder |
