# Wheat Flour Type 550

**Supplier grade:** soft/hard wheat blend, ash 0.51–0.63% (dry basis), protein 10.0–11.5%, wet gluten 26–29%, falling number 250–350 s, moisture ≤ 14.5%, farinograph water absorption 58–62%.

## Function

- **Gluten network:** gliadin and glutenin form an elastic network when mixed with water. It holds gas in yeast dough and gives bread its volume and chewy crumb.
- **Starch:** 68–72% starch gelatinises at 60–75 °C and sets the crumb together with the proteins.
- **Water binding:** absorbs 58–62% of its weight in water in bread dough.
- **Structure in cakes and cookies:** low-gluten mixing (short mixing, fat, sugar) keeps the texture tender.

## Typical dosage

| Product | Dosage |
|---|---|
| Yeast bread | 55–60% of dough (baker's percentage 100%) |
| Muffins, cakes | 25–35% of batter |
| Cookies | 40–50% of dough |
| Pancakes | 25–35% of batter |

## Allergens

Contains **gluten (wheat)**, EU 1169/2011 Annex II. Not suitable for gluten-free or coeliac products. May contain traces of soy and sesame from shared mill lines (see supplier "may contain" declaration).

## Substitutes for gluten-free products

| Substitute | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gluten-free flour blend (spec-gluten-free-blend) | 1:1 by weight + hydrocolloid | +10–25% water; see trial-gluten-free-bread |
| Rice flour alone | not recommended | gritty texture, crumbles; use only within a blend |
| Buckwheat flour | up to 30% of the blend | strong flavour; buckwheat is gluten-free and not an EU14 allergen |
| Oat flour | up to 20% | only if certified gluten-free oats |

Spelt, kamut, rye and barley flours are **not** gluten-free substitutes: they are all "cereals containing gluten".

## Cautions

- Gluten-free bread without a hydrocolloid (xanthan, psyllium) has 40–50% lower specific volume and crumbles.
- Hydrocolloid dosage on flour weight: cookies 0.3–0.5% xanthan, cakes and muffins 0.8–1.0%, bread 1.5–2% xanthan or 3–5% psyllium husk.
- Gluten-free doughs are batters: they cannot be kneaded or shaped by hand; use tins.
