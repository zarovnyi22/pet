# Unsalted Butter, 82% Fat

**Supplier grade:** sweet cream butter, minimum 82% milk fat, maximum 16% water, 1.5% non-fat milk solids. Stored at 2–6 °C; blocks for lamination kept at 10–12 °C before use.

## Function

- **Tenderness:** coats flour particles and limits gluten development ("shortening").
- **Aeration:** creaming with sugar at 18–20 °C traps air; this gives most of the volume in butter cakes and cookies.
- **Flavour and colour:** dairy aroma, milk solids add browning.
- **Spread:** melting range 32–35 °C controls cookie spread; butter cookies spread more than cookies made with shortening.
- **Lamination:** plastic layers in croissant and puff pastry at 10–15 °C.

## Typical dosage

| Product | Dosage |
|---|---|
| Butter cookies | 25–35% of dough |
| Pound and butter cakes | 20–25% of batter |
| Muffins | 12–18% of batter (often melted) |
| Pancakes | 3–6% melted |
| Croissant | 50–60% of flour weight for lamination |

## Substitutes

| Substitute | Ratio | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant-based block, 80% fat (shea, rapeseed, coconut) | 1:1 by weight | cookies, cakes, lamination | closest creaming and melting behaviour; check for soy lecithin |
| Coconut oil, refined | 0.82 g oil + 0.16 g water per 1 g butter | cookies, shortbread | firm below 24 °C; cookies spread 10–15% less |
| Rapeseed oil | 0.8 g per 1 g butter | muffins, pancakes, quick breads | no creaming possible — use only in "all-in" or melted-butter methods; used in trial-vegan-pancakes |

## Cautions

- Liquid oils cannot replace butter in creamed cakes: volume drops 20–30% because no air is trapped.
- When calculating nutrition for oil substitutes, remember butter has 16% water; oil has none, so reduce the oil amount as shown above.
- Some plant blocks contain milk-derived flavourings; confirm "vegan" on the supplier specification.
