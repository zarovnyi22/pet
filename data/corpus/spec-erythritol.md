# Erythritol (E968)

**Supplier grade:** crystalline erythritol from fermentation, purity ≥ 99.5%, particle size 0.2–0.8 mm. Polyol (sugar alcohol). Produced by fermentation of glucose from maize starch on plant-based media; no animal-derived inputs.

## Function

- **Sweetness:** 0.6–0.7 of sucrose. It is the only polyol labelled at **0 kcal/g** in the EU.
- **Bulk:** can replace sucrose 1:1 by weight, so batter mass and volume stay close to the original.
- **No browning:** erythritol does not take part in Maillard reactions or caramelisation. Products are paler; expect crust colour L* to be 3–6 units higher.
- **Cooling effect:** strong negative heat of solution (−43 cal/g). It is noticeable in soft products and fillings when erythritol is above 8% of product weight.
- **Crystallisation:** low solubility (about 37% at 25 °C). In moist products with erythritol above 15%, white crystals appear on the surface after 1–3 days.

## Typical dosage

| Product | Max erythritol (% of product) |
|---|---|
| Cookies, biscuits | 10–15% |
| Muffins, cakes | 5–8% (crystallisation above this) |
| Fillings, creams | 5% (cooling effect) |
| Beverages | 3–5% |

The usual approach is to replace **40–60% of the removed sugar mass** with erythritol and the rest with polydextrose (spec-polydextrose), and to make up sweetness with 0.01–0.03% steviol glycosides (spec-stevia).

## Labelling

- Declared as "sweetener (erythritol)" or E968.
- If added polyols are above **10%** of the product, the label must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" (EU 1169/2011 Annex III).
- Internal limit: maximum **15 g erythritol per serving**.

## Substitutes

- Isomalt (E953, 2.4 kcal/g) or maltitol (E965, 2.4 kcal/g) where browning is needed, but both add calories.
- Polydextrose for bulk without the cooling effect.

## Cautions

- Replacing sugar only with erythritol in muffins failed in trial-muffin-sugar-50: crystals by day 2 and cooling noticed by 9 of 12 panelists.
- Erythritol does not raise the gelatinisation temperature as much as sucrose; batters set earlier, so bake at 5–10 °C lower for a longer time.
